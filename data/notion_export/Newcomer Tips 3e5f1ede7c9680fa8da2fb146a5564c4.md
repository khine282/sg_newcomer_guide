# Newcomer Tips

Practical tips for people who just moved to Singapore, written from personal experience. Always check official websites for the latest rules.

## First week checklist

- Keep your pass (for example, Employment Pass, S Pass, Work Permit or Student Pass) and passport in a safe place, and carry your pass card when required.
- Get a local SIM card so you have a Singapore phone number. Many services (banks, delivery apps, one-time passwords) need one.
- Set up a way to pay for public transport.
- Save your home address in English exactly as written on your tenancy agreement; you will need it for forms.

## Getting a SIM card

- Main telcos: Singtel, StarHub, M1 and SIMBA, plus several cheaper online-only brands.
- You need your passport or pass to register a SIM card.
- Prepaid is easiest at the start; switch to a postpaid plan later if you want.

## Getting around

- You can pay for MRT and buses with a contactless bank card, a mobile wallet, or a stored value card like EZ-Link.
- Download the MyTransport app for bus arrival times, train timings and journey planning.
- Eating and drinking are not allowed on the MRT and in paid areas of stations.
- Tap your card when you board AND when you leave the bus, otherwise you may be charged the maximum fare.

## Opening a bank account

- Usually you need your passport, your pass, and proof of your Singapore address.
- Some banks let pass holders open an account through their mobile app; others need a branch visit.
- Ask whether there is a minimum balance or monthly fee before choosing.

## Finding a place to rent

- Renting a room in an HDB flat is the most common and affordable option for newcomers.
- To rent an HDB flat or room, non-citizens need a valid pass (for example, Employment Pass, S Pass, Work Permit, Student Pass, Dependant Pass or Long-Term Visit Pass). Check the HDB website for the exact conditions.
- If you use a property agent, check they are registered with CEA (Council for Estate Agencies).
- Always sign a written tenancy agreement and read it carefully (deposit, notice period, who pays utilities).
- Never transfer money before viewing the place and signing an agreement. Rental scams are common.

## Seeing a doctor

- For common illnesses, visit a GP (general practitioner) clinic or a polyclinic.
- Foreigners pay higher fees than citizens at polyclinics, so check the charges first.
- Check if your employer provides medical insurance or covers clinic visits.
- In an emergency, call 995 for an ambulance or go to a hospital A&E (Accident & Emergency).

## Common mistakes to avoid

- Clicking links in SMS messages claiming to be from banks or government agencies. Official agencies will not ask for your password or OTP.
- Jaywalking: use pedestrian crossings.
- Losing track of your pass expiry date.
- Signing a rental agreement without reading it.
